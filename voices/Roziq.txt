In 2022, the food writer and campaigner Jack Monroe pointed out that official inflation figures were tracking a basket of goods that did not reflect what the poorest households actually buy, and that the very cheapest items were rising in price far faster than the headline rate suggested. The campaign that followed became known as the Vimes Boots Index, and it pushed the Office for National Statistics to start tracking the price of the lowest cost items specifically. A line from a fantasy novel ended up changing how a national statistics agency measures the cost of living. That is how real this effect is, and it is worth being clear-eyed about why the cheap tier exists at all. It is not an accident, and it is certainly not charity. There is a reliable market of people who cannot pay once, and there is a product priced for them that will need buying again and again.
